CREATE A
JOY TREE

Happiness isn't a solo thing — it grows when you share it. Your mission is to build a living artwork that captures what lifts your whole class up.

Your Mission
🎯 What you're doing
Your class will create a large colourful paper tree together. Each student adds leaves with words, drawings, or moments that represent joy and positivity. By the end, you'll have a shared symbol of what makes your class flourish.
📦 What you need
Brown craft paper or cardboard (for the trunk) · Coloured construction paper (for leaves) · Scissors · Markers or pens · Tape or blu-tack · A wall big enough to hold the tree
⏱ Time
Around 25–35 minutes total — 5 min setup, 10 min making leaves, 10 min building the tree, 5–10 min debrief.
How to do it — Guidelines
1
Set up the tree first
Draw or tape a tree trunk with 5–6 branches onto a large sheet. Stick it on the wall at a reachable height before the activity starts. Branches should be empty — students fill them.
2
Colour-code your leaves
Use different leaf colours to represent different types of positivity. Suggested: Yellow = a joyful memory, Green = something you're grateful for, Blue = a person who makes you happy. Each student gets 2–3 leaves.
3
Write something real, not perfect
One word, a short phrase, or a quick drawing — anything that genuinely represents a positive moment or feeling for you. There are no wrong answers. Think: "When did I last feel genuinely good? What was happening?"
4
Add your leaf and say something
When you place your leaf on the tree, say one sentence out loud about what you wrote. You don't need to explain it fully — just name it. "This is the feeling I get when I finish something hard."
5
Stand back and notice
Once everyone has added their leaves, step back. What patterns do you see? What surprised you? What do many people have in common? These observations are your debrief — no facilitator needed.
Your Checklist
0 / 5
Tree base is set up on the wall with 5–6 branches
Leaves are cut out and colour-coded
Every student has written on their leaves
All leaves are added to the tree
Class debrief done — patterns and surprises discussed
🌳 Joy Tree complete! Leave it up for the rest of the programme.

CLASS ENGAGEMENT
PLAYBOOK

You know what keeps you focused better than anyone else does. This mission is about turning that knowledge into something your whole class can actually use.

Your Mission
🎯 What you're doing
In groups, you'll design an "Engagement Playbook" — a poster or booklet filled with rituals, practices, and activities that help your class stay energised, focused, and included. Think of it like a game plan for staying engaged all year.
📦 What you need
A3 paper or large cards · Markers · Dot stickers for voting · Groups of 4–5 students
⏱ Time
Around 35–45 minutes total — 5 min intro, 10 min brainstorm, 15 min design, 10 min gallery vote, 5 min commit.
How to do it — Guidelines
1
Form groups and pick roles
Groups of 4–5. Choose roles: Scribe (writes ideas), Designer (makes it look good), Presenter (shares with class), Idea Generator (keeps energy high). Roles can overlap.
2
Brainstorm your engagement ideas
Discuss 3 questions in your group: (1) What helps YOU personally focus in class? (2) What could instantly lift the energy when everyone's low? (3) What makes you actually want to participate? Write everything — no filters yet.
3
Structure your Playbook page into 3 zones
ENERGISE — rituals that boost the room's energy. FOCUS — habits that help everyone concentrate. INCLUDE — practices that make every student feel seen and part of things. Each idea gets a short punchy name.
4
Gallery walk and vote
Post all playbooks around the room. Do a silent walk with dot stickers. You have 3 stickers — place them on ideas you'd actually want the class to use. You can spread them or put all 3 on one idea you really believe in.
5
Commit to the top 3 as a class
Count the votes together. The top 3 ideas become your Class Engagement Pledge. Write them on a card, everyone signs it, and it goes up on the wall. You're now accountable to each other — in a good way.
Your Checklist
0 / 5
Groups formed and roles assigned
Brainstorm done — ideas for all 3 questions written down
Playbook page designed with 3 zones (Energise / Focus / Include)
Gallery walk done and votes counted
Top 3 ideas committed to and signed by the class
📖 Playbook complete! Now actually use those 3 ideas.

WEB OF
CONNECTIONS

Every person in this room is linked to at least one other person in a way they haven't discovered yet. Your mission is to make those invisible connections visible.

Your Mission
🎯 What you're doing
Using string and name tags, your class will physically build a web that links classmates through shared interests, qualities, and fun facts. The web you create becomes a map of your class's connections — real, visible, and surprising.
📦 What you need
A ball of yarn or string · Index cards or thick paper (name tags) · Markers · A hole punch · A camera to photograph the finished web
⏱ Time
Around 30–40 minutes — 10 min making name tags, 15 min building the web, 10 min debrief and photo.
How to do it — Guidelines
1
Make your name tag node
Write your name large on a card. Then draw or write 3 things: a hobby, a food you love, and a hidden talent (can be funny or unexpected). Punch a hole and loop a short piece of string through it — this is your node in the web.
2
Stand in a circle
Everyone stands in a circle holding their name tag card. One person starts with the ball of yarn, holds the end, and says: "I'm connecting to [name] because we both [shared thing]." Then passes the ball across the circle to that person.
3
Keep passing — build the web
Each person holds their section of yarn before passing the ball on. After 10–15 passes, a web forms in the centre of the circle. Every student must make at least one connection before the round ends.
4
Tug the web — feel the connection
When the web is built, one person gently tugs their section. Everyone holding the yarn will feel it. Then discuss: in a class, when one person struggles or rises, everyone feels it — even if they don't realise it.
5
Photograph and display
Take a wide or top-down photo of the web while students hold it up. Print it out, attach the name tags around it, and display it in the classroom. This web is your class for the year.
Your Checklist
0 / 5
Name tag nodes made — name, hobby, food, hidden talent
Class standing in a circle with yarn ready
Every student has made at least one connection out loud
Web tug moment done and discussed
Web photographed and displayed in the classroom
🕸️ Web complete! Every thread is a real connection.

CLASS VISION
SCROLL

What kind of class do you want to look back on at the end of the year? Your mission is to answer that question — together, in writing, in a way that actually sticks.

Your Mission
🎯 What you're doing
In groups, you'll draft and decorate a Vision Scroll — a long strip of paper that captures your class's shared purpose, values, and aspirations. At the end, the whole class co-writes a unified Vision Statement that everyone signs. This is your north star for the year.
📦 What you need
Long strips of kraft/brown paper (or taped A3 sheets) · Markers, pens, coloured pencils · Stickers and washi tape if available · Groups of 4–5
⏱ Time
Around 40–50 minutes — 5 min reflection, 10 min values discussion, 15 min scroll creation, 10 min presentations, 10 min unified statement.
How to do it — Guidelines
1
Start with silent reflection
Before talking to anyone, spend 2 minutes thinking: "What kind of class do I want to look back on at the end of the year?" Write down 1–2 thoughts privately first. This stops the loudest voice from dominating.
2
Agree on your group's 3–5 values
Share your reflections and agree on 3–5 values your group believes this class should stand for. Use the word bank if helpful: Respect · Curiosity · Courage · Kindness · Excellence · Fun · Growth · Inclusion · Honesty · Creativity — or invent your own.
3
Design your scroll
Write your values in large letters on the scroll. Add symbols, quotes, and drawings that represent each value. Title it: "WE ARE [YOUR CLASS] AND WE STAND FOR..." Make it look like it matters — because it does.
4
Present and find common ground
Each group unrolls their scroll and shares. Look for values that appear across multiple groups — those are your strongest candidates for the class vision. Notice when different words mean the same thing.
5
Write the unified Vision Statement
Together as a class, write 1–3 sentences starting with: "As [class name], we commit to..." Every student signs it. Display it where it can be read. Return to it at key moments throughout the year.
Your Checklist
0 / 5
Silent reflection done — personal thoughts written down first
Group has agreed on 3–5 values with reasons for each
Vision Scroll designed and decorated
All groups have presented and common values identified
Unified Class Vision Statement written and signed by everyone
📜 Vision locked in! Your class knows what it stands for.

CLASS
TROPHY WALL

Winning isn't the only kind of accomplishment. Showing up, trying something hard, helping someone — these count too. Your mission is to make every student's growth visible.

Your Mission
🎯 What you're doing
You'll design a Trophy Wall — a dedicated display that showcases every student's individual milestones and achievements. This isn't just about grades. It's about recognising every kind of growth, including the ones that usually go unnoticed.
📦 What you need
A5 Trophy Card templates (printed or handmade) · Sticky note stars or small stickers · Markers · A dedicated wall section with a banner header
⏱ Time
Around 35–45 minutes — 10 min redefining wins, 10 min making Trophy Cards, 8 min Shout-Out Round, 7 min building the wall, 5–10 min closing ritual.
How to do it — Guidelines
1
Redefine what "winning" means
Before anything else, have a class discussion: What counts as an accomplishment? Challenge the idea that only grades and prizes count. Add answers to a list on the board — speaking up in class, helping a classmate, finishing something you almost gave up, trying something new all count.
2
Fill in your Trophy Card
On your A5 card, write: (1) One thing you've already accomplished this term — no matter how small. (2) One thing you're currently working toward. (3) Draw a personal trophy, medal, or symbol that represents your win. Make it yours.
3
The Shout-Out Round
Each student gets 2 sticky note stars. Anonymously write a genuine recognition for a classmate: "I think [name] deserves a trophy for..." Be specific. Stick it to their card. No generic compliments — name something real you've actually noticed.
4
Build the Trophy Wall display
Post all cards on a dedicated wall section with the banner: "WE GROW. WE ACHIEVE. WE CELEBRATE." Arrange neatly. Leave space on each card — this wall should grow over the year as new wins get added.
5
Close with a moment that marks it
Everyone stands. Facilitator: "Raise your hand if someone recognised something in you today that you didn't know others noticed." Let that sit. Close with a class clap, cheer, or fist-bump — whatever feels natural. The goal is to mark the moment as meaningful.
Your Checklist
0 / 5
Class discussion done — expanded definition of "accomplishment" on board
Every student has filled in their Trophy Card (past win + current goal + symbol)
Shout-Out Round done — everyone has received at least one peer recognition
Trophy Wall is built and displayed
Closing ritual done — the moment is marked
🏆 Trophy Wall is live! Every student has something worth celebrating.
