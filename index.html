CREATE A
JOY TREE

Happiness isn't a solo thing — it grows when you share it. Build a living artwork that captures what lifts your whole class up.

Your Mission
What you're doing
Your class will build a large paper tree together on the wall. Each student creates leaves filled with words, drawings, or moments that represent joy, gratitude, and positivity. Every leaf goes on the tree with a sentence shared out loud. By the end, you'll have a shared living symbol of what makes your class flourish — and it stays up for the rest of the year.
By the end of this activity, your class will have
  • A colourful tree on the wall filled with every student's positive moments
  • Named the feelings and people that genuinely lift each of you up
  • Discovered surprising things you have in common with classmates
How to do it
1
Set up the tree base on the wall
Draw or tape a tree trunk with 5–6 spreading branches onto large brown craft paper. Stick it to a wall everyone can reach. The branches should be completely empty — your class fills them.
2
Cut out your colourful leaves
Each student gets 2–3 leaf-shaped cutouts in different colours. Colour-code them: Yellow = a joyful memory, Green = something you're grateful for, Blue = a person who makes you happy.
3
Write something real, not perfect
On each leaf, write one word, a short phrase, or draw a quick symbol. Ask yourself: "When did I last feel genuinely good? What was happening?" There are no wrong answers here.
4
Place your leaf and say something
When you stick your leaf on the tree, say one sentence out loud about what you wrote. You don't need to explain it deeply — just name it. "This is the feeling I get when I finish something hard."
5
Step back and notice patterns
Once everyone's leaves are on, step back together. What do you see? What surprised you? What do many people have in common? These observations are your debrief — they're the actual lesson.
Activity Checklist
0 / 5
Tree base is up on the wall with 5–6 empty branches
Leaves are cut out and colour-coded by category
Every student has written or drawn on their leaves
All leaves are on the tree — each person shared a sentence
Class stood back and discussed patterns and surprises
🌳 Joy Tree complete! Leave it up for the rest of the programme.

CLASS ENGAGEMENT
PLAYBOOK

You know what keeps you focused better than anyone else does. Turn that knowledge into something your whole class can actually use all year.

Your Mission
What you're doing
In groups, you'll design a Class Engagement Playbook — a poster packed with rituals, habits, and activities that help your class stay energised, focused, and included. Think of it as a game plan your whole class co-creates and owns. After every group designs their page, the class votes on the best ideas, and the top 3 become a pledge everyone commits to together.
By the end of this activity, your class will have
  • A class-owned set of engagement rituals you actually designed
  • Voted on and committed to 3 practices to use all year
  • Understood what keeps each other focused, energised, and included
How to do it
1
Form groups and assign roles
Groups of 4–5. Pick roles: Scribe (writes ideas down), Designer (makes it look good), Presenter (shares with class), Idea Generator (keeps energy flowing). Roles can overlap — choose what fits you.
2
Brainstorm with 3 questions
Discuss these in your group — no filtering, everything goes on paper first: (1) What helps you personally focus in class? (2) What could instantly lift the energy when everyone's low? (3) What makes you actually want to participate?
3
Design your Playbook with 3 zones
On A3 paper, organise your ideas into: ENERGISE (rituals that boost the room), FOCUS (habits that help everyone concentrate), INCLUDE (practices that make every student feel part of it). Give each idea a short punchy name — like a move in a game.
4
Gallery walk and vote
Post all Playbooks around the room. Everyone does a silent gallery walk with 3 dot stickers. Vote for ideas you'd actually want the class to use. You can spread your stickers or put all 3 on one idea you feel strongly about.
5
Lock in the top 3 as a class
Count votes together. The top 3 ideas become your Class Engagement Pledge. Write them on a card, everyone signs it, and it goes up next to the Joy Tree. You're now accountable to each other — in the best way.
Activity Checklist
0 / 5
Groups formed and roles chosen
Brainstorm done — ideas for all 3 questions written down
Playbook designed with 3 zones (Energise / Focus / Include)
Gallery walk done and votes tallied
Top 3 ideas on a pledge card — signed by everyone
📖 Playbook complete! Now actually use those 3 ideas.

WEB OF
CONNECTIONS

Every person in this room is linked to at least one other in a way they haven't discovered yet. Your mission is to make those invisible threads visible.

Your Mission
What you're doing
Using yarn and name tags, your class will physically build a web in the centre of a circle — connecting classmates through shared interests, qualities, and fun facts. Each connection is said out loud before the string is passed on. By the end, you'll have a real, visible map of your class's relationships, and you'll physically feel what happens when one part of the web moves.
By the end of this activity, your class will have
  • Discovered surprising connections you didn't know existed
  • Physically felt how linked every person in the room is
  • A class photo of the web to display all year
How to do it
1
Make your name tag node
Write your name large on a card. Add 3 things: a hobby, a food you love, and a hidden talent (funny or real). Punch a hole and loop a short string through — this card is your node in the web.
2
Stand in a circle and start the web
Everyone stands in a circle holding their card. One person starts with the ball of yarn, holds the end, and says: "I'm connecting to [name] because we both [shared thing]." Then pass the ball across to that person.
3
Keep passing — build the web
Each person holds their section before tossing the ball on to someone new. After 10–15 passes, a web forms in the centre of the circle. Every student makes at least one connection before the round ends.
4
Tug the web — feel the connection
With everyone holding the web, one person gently tugs their section. Everyone holding the yarn feels it. Then reflect together: in a class, when one person struggles or rises, everyone feels it — even if they don't realise it.
5
Photograph it and put it up
Take a wide photo of the web while everyone holds it. Print it, attach the name cards around it, and display it in the classroom. This web is your class for the year.
Activity Checklist
0 / 5
Name tag nodes made — name, hobby, food, hidden talent
Everyone is standing in a circle with yarn ready
Every student has made at least one connection out loud
Web tug moment done and discussed as a class
Web photographed and displayed in the classroom
🕸️ Web complete! Every thread is a real connection.

CLASS VISION
SCROLL

What kind of class do you want to look back on at the end of the year? Your mission is to answer that — together, in writing, in a way that actually sticks.

Your Mission
What you're doing
In groups, you'll draft and decorate a Vision Scroll — a long strip of paper capturing your class's shared values, purpose, and aspirations. After each group presents, the whole class finds common ground and co-writes a single unified Vision Statement that every student signs. This becomes your class's north star — something to return to at key moments all year.
By the end of this activity, your class will have
  • Identified the values your class genuinely stands for
  • A decorated Vision Scroll created by students, not handed down
  • A unified class Vision Statement signed by everyone
How to do it
1
Start with 2 minutes of silent thinking
Before talking to anyone, write your personal answer to: "What kind of class do I want to look back on at the end of the year?" Writing first stops the loudest voice from shaping everyone else's thinking.
2
Agree on 3–5 group values
Share your reflections and agree on 3–5 values your group believes this class should stand for. Word bank if helpful: Respect · Curiosity · Courage · Kindness · Excellence · Fun · Growth · Inclusion · Honesty · Creativity. For each value, name why you chose it — the why is where the meaning lives.
3
Design and decorate your scroll
Write your values in large letters on a long strip of paper. Add symbols, quotes, and drawings. Title it: "WE ARE [YOUR CLASS] AND WE STAND FOR..." Make it look like something that matters — because it does.
4
Present and find common ground
Each group unrolls and shares. Look for values that appear across multiple groups — those are your strongest candidates for the class vision. Notice when different words mean the same thing.
5
Write the unified Vision Statement together
As a full class, write 1–3 sentences starting with: "As [class name], we commit to..." Every student signs the scroll. Display it where it can be read. Return to it at key moments throughout the year.
Activity Checklist
0 / 5
Silent reflection done — personal answers written down first
Group agreed on 3–5 values with a reason for each one
Vision Scroll designed, decorated and titled
All groups presented — common values identified across scrolls
Unified Class Vision Statement written and signed by everyone
📜 Vision locked in! Your class knows what it stands for.

✦ Accomplishment

CLASS
TROPHY WALL

Winning isn't the only kind of accomplishment. Showing up, trying something hard, helping someone — these count too. Make every student's growth visible.

Your Mission
What you're doing
You'll design a Trophy Wall — a dedicated classroom display that showcases every student's individual milestones and achievements. This activity starts by redefining what a win actually is — beyond grades and prizes. Then every student makes a personal Trophy Card, gives anonymous peer recognition, and the whole class builds the wall together before closing with a ritual that marks the moment.
By the end of this activity, your class will have
  • Expanded what "accomplishment" means beyond academic results
  • Every student's wins — past and current — displayed on the wall
  • Received genuine anonymous recognition from a classmate
How to do it
1
Redefine what "winning" means
Have a class discussion: "What counts as an accomplishment?" Push past grades and trophies. Speaking up in class, helping a classmate, finishing something you almost gave up — all of these count. Write the expanded list on the board before anyone starts their card.
2
Fill in your Trophy Card
On your A5 card, write: (1) One thing you've already accomplished this term — no matter how small. (2) One thing you're currently working toward. (3) Draw a personal trophy, medal, or symbol that represents your win. Make it yours.
3
The Shout-Out Round
Each student gets 2 sticky note stars. Anonymously write a genuine recognition for a classmate: "I think [name] deserves a trophy for..." Be specific — name something real you've actually noticed. Stick it to their card. No generic compliments.
4
Build the Trophy Wall
Post all cards on a dedicated wall section with the banner: "WE GROW. WE ACHIEVE. WE CELEBRATE." Arrange them neatly and leave space on each card — this wall grows over the year as new wins get added.
5
Close with a moment that marks it
Everyone stands. Facilitator: "Raise your hand if someone recognised something in you today that you didn't know others noticed." Let that sit. Close with a class clap, cheer, or fist-bump — whatever feels natural. The goal is to mark the moment as meaningful.
Activity Checklist
0 / 5
Class discussion done — expanded definition of "accomplishment" on the board
Every student has filled their Trophy Card (past win + goal + symbol)
Shout-Out Round done — everyone has at least one peer recognition
Trophy Wall is built and displayed with the banner
Closing ritual done — the moment is marked
🏆 Trophy Wall is live! Every student has something worth celebrating.
